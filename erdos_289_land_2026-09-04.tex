\title{A solution of Erd\H{o}s Problem 289\\ with intervals of length two and three}
\author{Johan Land\\ \small Independent Researcher}
\date{4 September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Erd\H{o}s and Graham asked whether, for every sufficiently large integer $k$, there exist $k$ finite intervals of positive integers, each containing at least two integers and no two of them overlapping or adjacent, such that the reciprocals of all integers in the intervals sum to $1$. We prove a stronger statement: for every sufficiently large $k$ the intervals can be chosen to have length two or three and to lie in $[1,20k]$. The proof adapts prime-power absorption to signed pairs of consecutive integers. Complementary modular inverses and an elementary sieve provide large correction fibers, and a finite orientation argument makes these fibers simultaneously nonadjacent. A sparse inverse-covering lemma, obtained from the Conlon--Fox--Pham structure theorem and a divisor-count argument, enables a descending denominator correction with a predetermined interval count. A protected core is completed using the Liu--Sawhney theorem. The accompanying Lean formalization proves the stated target with no custom axioms in its dependency chain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem and the main theorem}\label{sec:problem}

Problem 289 on the Erd\H{o}s problems site \cite{EP289}, originating in Erd\H{o}s and Graham \cite{EG80}, reads as follows.

\begin{quote}
Is it true that, for all sufficiently large $k$, there exist finite intervals $I_1,\ldots,I_k\subset\N$, distinct, not overlapping or adjacent, with $|I_i|\ge2$ for $1\le i\le k$, such that
\[
1=\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{n\in I_i}\frac1n\,?
\]
\end{quote}

Throughout, an interval of integers $[a,b]$ with $a\le b$ consists of all integers $n$ with $a\le n\le b$, so $|[a,b]|=b-a+1$. Two intervals $[a,b]$ and $[a',b']$ with $b<a'$ are \emph{adjacent} if $b+1=a'$, and \emph{separated} if $b+1<a'$, that is, if at least one integer lies strictly between them. A family of intervals is separated if any two of its members are separated after ordering them.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every sufficiently large integer $k$ there are integer intervals $[a_i,b_i]$, $1\le i\le k$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
2\le a_i\le b_i\le20k,\qquad b_i-a_i+1\in\{2,3\},\qquad
b_i+1<a_{i+1}\ \ (1\le i<k),\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_{n=a_i}^{b_i}\frac1n=1 .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:289}
The answer to Problem 289 is affirmative: for all sufficiently large $k$ there exist finite intervals $I_1,\ldots,I_k\subset\N$, distinct, not overlapping or adjacent, with $|I_i|\ge2$, such that $1=\sum_i\sum_{n\in I_i}1/n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $I_i=[a_i,b_i]$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. For $i<j$ the inequalities $b_i+1<a_{i+1}\le a_j$ show that $I_i$ and $I_j$ are disjoint, hence distinct, and that the integer $b_i+1$ lies in neither of them, so they are not adjacent. Each $|I_i|\in\{2,3\}$ is at least $2$, and the reciprocal sum is the same.
\end{proof}

The separation requirement is the substance of the problem. Allowing adjacency would permit splitting an interval of length at least four into adjacent pieces of lengths two and three, increasing the interval count without changing the union or its reciprocal mass; and as observed in the discussion of \cite{EP289}, without the restrictions the question is easy. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} says more than the problem asks: the intervals have length two or three, and all of them lie in $[1,20k]$. The constant $20$ is not optimized; it arises from the far band $[4k,20k]$ used in Section~\ref{sec:main}, where the only requirement is the inequality $16\gamma>1$ in \eqref{eq:gammabounds}.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:two}
The condition $a_i\ge2$ in \eqref{eq:main} is automatic once the other conditions hold. If some interval began at $1$, its length condition would make it contain both $1$ and $2$, so its reciprocal mass alone would be at least $1+\tfrac12>1$, while every other interval has positive mass; this contradicts total mass one. The formal target in Appendix~\ref{app:cert} requires only $a_i\ge1$, and this observation bridges the two formulations. The construction below also gives $a_i\ge2$ directly.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Outline of the proof}\label{sec:outline}

The proof adapts the absorption argument in Section~4 of Conlon, Fox, He, Mubayi, Pham, Suk and Verstra\"ete \cite{CFHMPSV}. Its descending cancellation of denominator prime powers, its small reciprocal budget, and its terminal reservoir form the starting architecture, and Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} below is a sparse version of that paper's inverse-covering theorem, proved by the same bounded-rank progression and divisor-count method. The new correction mechanism uses pairs of consecutive integers with complementary signs, a finite orientation selection that makes them simultaneously nonadjacent, a fixed number of pairs at each correction stage, and a core completed by extending pairs to triples.

The final family consists of intervals of three kinds, living at three different scales (Figure~\ref{fig:line}).

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Core pairs} $[Kd+1,Kd+2]$, where $K$ is a fixed integer and $d$ runs over a set $\cR$ of parameters of size $\Theta(k^{4/5})$ (Section~\ref{sec:core}). Some of these pairs will later be extended to the triples $[Kd,Kd+2]$.
\item \textbf{Main pairs} $[a,a+1]$ with $3\mid a$, lying in $[k/1024,k]\cup[4k,20k]$, all of whose endpoints are $k^{4/5}$-powersmooth (Section~\ref{sec:main}). There are exactly $k-|\cR|-C_H$ of them, where $C_H$ is a number fixed in advance, and they are chosen so that the \emph{initial deficit}
\[
r_0=1-(\text{mass of core})-(\text{mass of main pairs})
\]
is a rational number of size about $\delta/2$, with $\delta=1/1000$, whose denominator has all prime-power divisors at most $H=O(k^{4/5})$.
\item \textbf{Correction and auxiliary pairs}, with endpoints between $L$ and $8H^{11/10}+1=O(k^{22/25})$, added by a descent (Section~\ref{sec:descent}) which cancels the prime powers of the denominator of $r_0$ one at a time, in decreasing order.
\end{enumerate}

The descent works as follows. Let $q=p^a$ be the largest prime power in the current denominator. A \emph{signed correction pair} at $q$ is a pair $\{qm,qm+\sigma\}$ of consecutive integers, with a multiplier $m$ coprime to $p$ and a sign $\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}$, such that the neighbour $qm+\sigma$ has all its prime-power divisors below $q$. Its mass is
\[
w_\sigma(qm)=\frac1{qm}+\frac1{qm+\sigma},
\]
and modulo $q$ the first term behaves like $m^{-1}/q$ while the second is a junk term whose denominator involves only smaller prime powers. Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber} produces, by an elementary construction, a fiber of about $q^{\varepsilon}$ such multipliers: for each auxiliary parameter $t$ the two complementary multipliers $m_1$ and $m_2$ with $m_1+m_2=t$ (or $4t$) satisfy $qm_1\pm1=rt$ and $qm_2\mp1=(q-r)t$ for a modular inverse $r$ (with $t$ replaced by $4t$ throughout in the odd-prime case), so one of them is coprime to $p$, and a sieve on $t$ together with the divisor bound makes the neighbour smooth. No equidistribution of modular inverses is needed. Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} shows that the inverses of a sparse set of size $q^{7\varepsilon/8}$ already cover every residue modulo $q$ using at most $q^{\varepsilon/2}$ summands. Subtracting the corresponding pair masses therefore removes the factor $p^a$ from the denominator while introducing only smaller prime powers. This is the obstacle peculiar to pairs, as opposed to single unit fractions: the junk term $1/(qm+\sigma)$ must not reintroduce large prime powers.

Signed pairs at different labels could overlap or touch. Each pair has one endpoint that is a multiple of four, its \emph{position}, and is either the left pair $[y-1,y]$ or the right pair $[y,y+1]$ at that position $y$. Lemma~\ref{lem:orient} chooses, for all the finitely many prime powers $q\le H$ at once, one orientation per position by a finite counting argument, and keeps at least a quarter of every fiber; the retained pairs are then pairwise separated across all labels. This choice is finite and is made after $H$ is known.

To make the total number of intervals predetermined, Lemma~\ref{lem:aux} reserves, for each prime power $q$, a family $\cF_q$ of exactly $s(q)=\lfloor q^{1/20}\rfloor$ \emph{auxiliary} pairs whose endpoints are $(q/2)$-powersmooth. If the cancellation at $q$ uses $c\le s(q)$ correction pairs, $s(q)-c$ auxiliary pairs are added as well. Every stage then adds exactly $s(q)$ intervals, and the whole descent adds exactly $C_H=\sum_{L<q\le H}s(q)$ intervals. No interval is ever split or merged.

After the descent the residual deficit is $j/K$ for some integer $1\le j\le j_0$, with $j_0$ fixed. It is absorbed by extending core pairs to triples: extending $[Kd+1,Kd+2]$ to $[Kd,Kd+2]$ adds $1/(Kd)$, and the Liu--Sawhney theorem (through Lemma~\ref{lem:dense}) supplies, inside each of $j$ disjoint bands of parameters, a set $D$ with $\sum_{d\in D}1/d=1$. This adds exactly $j/K$ without changing the number of intervals.

Separation is arranged at each scale: retained correction pairs are separated by Lemma~\ref{lem:orient}, auxiliary pairs are chosen at distance at least two from every possible signed correction endpoint (Lemma~\ref{lem:aux}), core parameters $d$ are only used when the neighbourhood $[Kd-1,Kd+3]$ avoids every possible correction and auxiliary endpoint, and main pairs lie on a grid of spacing three, far beyond every other endpoint. The auxiliary families and the core are protected against a deterministic superset $\cP^*_L$ of all possible signed correction endpoints, which has density zero (Proposition~\ref{prop:density}), so they do not depend on the finite orientation choice. The final assembly in Section~\ref{sec:proof} verifies the count, the mass and the conditions in \eqref{eq:main}. Figure~\ref{fig:deps} shows how the ingredients depend on one another.

An earlier draft of this paper obtained the correction fibers from the Bourgain--Garaev bound for short sums of modular inverses \cite{BG} and the Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an inequality. The signed construction of Section~\ref{sec:fibers} removes that input entirely; the structure theorem of Section~\ref{sec:CF} is still used.

\subsection{Notation and conventions}\label{sec:notation}

An integer is \emph{$y$-powersmooth} if every prime-power divisor of it is at most $y$, and \emph{strictly $q$-powersmooth} if every prime-power divisor of it is less than $q$; for integral $q$ the latter is $(q-1)$-powersmoothness. The largest prime-power divisor of an integer always refers to numerical size. For a positive integer $a$ write
\[
w(a)=\frac1a+\frac1{a+1},
\]
and for a signed correction pair, with $qm\ge2$ and $\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}$,
\[
w_\sigma(qm)=\frac1{qm}+\frac1{qm+\sigma},\qquad
J(q,m,\sigma)=[\min(qm,qm+\sigma),\ \max(qm,qm+\sigma)].
\]
The \emph{mass} of a set of pairwise disjoint intervals is the sum of the reciprocals of all integers in them; thus the pair $[a,a+1]$ has mass $w(a)$ and $J(q,m,\sigma)$ has mass $w_\sigma(qm)$. All logarithms are natural, except for the explicitly written $\log_2$ in the structure theorem and its application. $\pi(x)$ counts primes up to $x$, and $\tau(n)$ is the number of divisors of $n$. A prime power is an integer $p^a$ with $p$ prime and $a\ge1$; sums and unions over $q$ ``prime power'' in a range run over all such integers in that range. Asymptotic notation $o(\cdot)$ and $O(\cdot)$ refers to $q\to\infty$, $X\to\infty$ or $k\to\infty$, as indicated, with every constant of Section~\ref{sec:params} held fixed. The symbol $M_q=q^\varepsilon$ is used for the fiber scale, and $M=k-R-C_H$ for the number of main pairs; the function $R(q)$ of Section~\ref{sec:envelope} is distinct from the core count $R=|\cR|$.

\section{External inputs and choice of parameters}\label{sec:inputs}

The proof uses two theorems from the literature and three classical estimates. Each is stated here in the form in which it is used, together with the place where its hypotheses are verified.

\subsection{Structure in bounded subset sums}\label{sec:CF}

A \emph{represented} generalized arithmetic progression of rank $D$ is given by integer generators $d_1,\ldots,d_D$ and real bounds $\alpha_i\le\beta_i$; for $t>0$ its \emph{dilate at scale $t$} is
\[
tP=\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^Dn_id_i:\ n_i\in\Z,\ t\alpha_i\le n_i\le t\beta_i\Bigr\},
\]
and $P=1P$. The generators are retained under dilation; only the coordinate intervals expand. For noninteger $t$ this depends on the representation, which is kept fixed throughout. The dilate $tP$ is \emph{proper} if its coordinate box $\prod_i([t\alpha_i,t\beta_i]\cap\Z)$ is nonempty and distinct coordinate vectors in it give distinct integers. Properness here includes nonemptiness: an empty coordinate box would be vacuously injective, and the face argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} needs an integer point to fix the inactive coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Conlon--Fox--Pham {\cite[Theorem 1.5]{CFP}}; Conlon--Fox--He--Mubayi--Pham--Suk--Verstra\"ete {\cite[Theorem 3]{CFHMPSV}}]\label{thm:CF}
Given fixed $\beta>1$ and $0<\eta<1$, there are constants $c>0$, $d_0$ and $m_0\ge2$ with the following property. If $m\ge m_0$, $A\subseteq[1,n]$ with $|A|=m$, $n\le m^\beta$, and
\[
m^\eta\le s\le\frac{cm}{\log_2m},
\]
then there are a represented progression $P$ of rank at most $d_0$, a set $J\subseteq A$, and a set $J'\subseteq J$, such that
\[
|J|\ge m-\frac{s\log_2m}c,\qquad J\cup\{0\}\subseteq P,\qquad |J'|\le s,
\]
$P$ and $csP$ are proper (in particular nonempty), and the subset sums of $J'$ contain a translate of $csP$, with dilation taken in the supplied representation.
\end{theorem}

The logarithms in Theorem~\ref{thm:CF} are to base $2$, following the convention of \cite{CFP}; the change of base only affects the constant $c$. Nonemptiness of the dilate is part of what the source proves: the representation supplied in the proof of \cite[Theorem 1.5, pp.~26--27]{CFP} is centered, and a centered coordinate box contains the zero vector after every positive dilation. We cite the numbering of the journal version of \cite{CFHMPSV}, which agrees with arXiv version 2; the numbering differs in the first arXiv version. Theorem~\ref{thm:CF} is used only in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}.

\subsection{Dense sets contain a unit-fraction sum of one}\label{sec:LS}

\begin{theorem}[Liu--Sawhney {\cite[Theorem 1.3]{LS}}]\label{thm:LS}
For any fixed $0<\zeta<1/2$, every sufficiently large $N$ and every $A\subseteq[1,N]$ with
\[
|A|\ge(1-1/e+\zeta)N
\]
admit $D\subseteq A$ with $\sum_{d\in D}1/d=1$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:LS} is used only in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dense}.

\subsection{Elementary estimates}\label{sec:elem}

We use Chebyshev's bound $\pi(x)\ll x/\log x$, Mertens' estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mertens}
\sum_{p\le x}\frac1p=\log\log x+B_1+o(1)
\end{equation}
for a constant $B_1$, and the divisor bound: for every fixed $\xi>0$, $\tau(n)\le n^{\xi}$ for all sufficiently large $n$; see \cite[Theorems 7, 427 and 315]{HW}. The divisor bound is applied to integers of size up to a constant times $q^{1+\varepsilon}$, and it is convenient to state the uniform form once.

\begin{proposition}[divisor envelope]\label{prop:envelope}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$, $c_0\ge1$ and $\xi>0$. For all sufficiently large $q$,
\[
\max_{1\le n\le\lceil c_0q^{1+\varepsilon}\rceil}\tau(n)\le q^{\xi}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $\xi'=\xi/(2(1+\varepsilon))$ and let $N_0$ be such that $\tau(n)\le n^{\xi'}$ for $n\ge N_0$. For $n<N_0$, $\tau(n)\le n<N_0\le q^\xi$ once $q$ is large. For $N_0\le n\le c_0q^{1+\varepsilon}+1\le2c_0q^{1+\varepsilon}$,
\[
\tau(n)\le(2c_0)^{\xi'}q^{(1+\varepsilon)\xi'}=(2c_0)^{\xi'}q^{\xi/2}\le q^\xi
\]
for large $q$. Different applications below use different values of $\xi$; each is a separate instance of this proposition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Choice of parameters}\label{sec:params}

All constants are chosen once, in the following order, and are independent of $k$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item $\varepsilon=1/10$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:fiber}, \ref{lem:orient} and \ref{lem:cover} and Proposition~\ref{prop:density} are proved for every fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$; only this value is used afterwards.
\item $\delta=1/1000$, the approximate size of the initial deficit.
\item An initial cutoff $L_{\rm base}$, large enough that the signed construction of Section~\ref{sec:fibers} is defined for all prime powers $q>L_{\rm base}$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:density} holds for the endpoint set $\cP^*_{L_{\rm base}}$. Since $\cP^*_L\subseteq\cP^*_{L_{\rm base}}$ for every $L\ge L_{\rm base}$, the density statement and the auxiliary-supply threshold derived from it in Lemma~\ref{lem:aux} apply uniformly to every later cutoff. This avoids choosing a threshold that depends on the cutoff it must bound.
\item An integer $L\ge L_{\rm base}$, so large that all of the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $L\ge11$.
\item[(b)] Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber} applies to every prime power $q>L$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:orient} applies with cutoff $L$ (that is, $L\ge L_0$ there).
\item[(c)] Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} applies with $C=8$ to every prime power $q>L$.
\item[(d)] The size conditions of the signed construction hold for $q>L$: $q>8q^{1/10}$, $R(q)\ge1$ and $E(q)\ge1$, with $R$ and $E$ as in Section~\ref{sec:envelope}.
\item[(e)] The divisor-envelope thresholds used in the sieve of Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber} hold for $q>L$.
\item[(f)] $E(q)\le q^{1/40}$ for every $q>L$.
\item[(g)] Lemma~\ref{lem:aux} applies with cutoff $L$.
\item[(h)] $120L^{-1/40}<\delta/16$.
\item[(i)] $K:=\lcm(1,\ldots,L)$ satisfies $K\delta\ge2$ and $K\ge8$.
\end{enumerate}
Each condition holds for all sufficiently large $L$, so a single choice of $L$ meets all of them; condition (a) alone is not sufficient for the construction.
\item $K=\lcm(1,\ldots,L)$, $j_0=\lceil K\delta\rceil$, and $B=4^{j_0}$.
\item The deterministic endpoint set $\cP^*_L$ of Section~\ref{sec:Pstar}, and for every prime power $q>L$ a family $\cF_q$ of $s(q)=\lfloor q^{1/20}\rfloor$ auxiliary pairs from Lemma~\ref{lem:aux}; these determine $\cF^*$ and $\cU=\cP^*_L\cup\cF^*$. The individual signed fibers $A_q$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber} may also be fixed now; no globally compatible infinite family of oriented pairs is required or asserted.
\end{enumerate}

The quantities depending on $k$ are then determined in this order:
\begin{gather*}
Q=\lfloor k^{4/5}\rfloor,\qquad H=KBQ+2,\qquad
C_H=\sum_{\substack{L<q\le H\\ q\ \text{prime power}}}s(q),\\
\text{the compatible fibers $(I_q,\sigma_q)_{L<q\le H}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:orient} for this $H$},\\
\cR\ \text{and}\ R=|\cR|\ \text{(Section~\ref{sec:core})},\qquad M=k-R-C_H .
\end{gather*}
The core $\cR$ avoids all of $\cP^*_L$ and therefore does not depend on the orientation choice; the two may be made in either order. The phrase ``for sufficiently large $k$'' is used at finitely many places, each time with a threshold depending only on the constants above. The argument yields no explicit value of that threshold, and the constants $K$ and $B$, as well as the thresholds implicit in Theorems~\ref{thm:CF} and \ref{thm:LS}, are extremely large.

\section{Signed correction fibers}\label{sec:fibers}

Throughout this section $0<\varepsilon<1$ is fixed and $q=p^a$ is a prime power tending to infinity.

\subsection{The divisor envelope and the multiplier range}\label{sec:envelope}

For sufficiently large integers $q$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
M_q=q^\varepsilon,\qquad
V(q)=\max_{1\le n\le\lceil25q^{1+\varepsilon}\rceil}\tau(n),\qquad
E(q)=V(q)^2\log q,\qquad
R(q)=\frac{q^\varepsilon}{E(q)} .
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:envelope}, $V(q)\le q^{\xi}$ for every fixed $\xi>0$ and large $q$; hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelopesize}
V(q)=q^{o(1)},\qquad E(q)=q^{o(1)},\qquad R(q)=q^{\varepsilon-o(1)}\longrightarrow\infty .
\end{equation}
The factor $\log q$ in $E(q)$ ensures $E(q)\to\infty$ without a separate argument that $V(q)$ does. The constant $25$ is a harmless envelope constant: every integer to which $V(q)$ is applied below is a neighbour $qm+\sigma$ with $1\le m\le8M_q$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelopecontain}
qm+\sigma\le8qM_q+1\le9q^{1+\varepsilon}\le\lceil25q^{1+\varepsilon}\rceil .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Individually valid signed pairs}\label{sec:fiberconstr}

\begin{lemma}[signed fibers]\label{lem:fiber}
For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$ there is $q_0$ such that every prime power $q=p^a\ge q_0$ admits a finite set $A_q$ of positive multipliers, signs $\sigma_q(m)\in\{-1,+1\}$ and positions $y_q(m)\in4\N$, $m\in A_q$, satisfying
\begin{gather}
|A_q|\ge\frac{M_q}{8V(q)},\qquad R(q)\le m\le8M_q,\qquad p\nmid m,\label{eq:S2}\\
qm+\sigma_q(m)\ \text{is strictly $q$-powersmooth},\label{eq:S3}\\
J(q,m,\sigma_q(m))\in\bigl\{[y_q(m)-1,y_q(m)],\ [y_q(m),y_q(m)+1]\bigr\}.\label{eq:S4}
\end{gather}
Within each fiber the positions $y_q(m)$, $m\in A_q$, are distinct. The largest prime power dividing either endpoint of $J(q,m,\sigma_q(m))$ is exactly $q$. Nothing is asserted about separation between pairs of different fibers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Initial parameters.} Let
\[
\cT_q=\{t\in[M_q,2M_q]\cap\N:\ \gcd(t,2p)=1\}.
\]
The density of integers coprime to $2p$ is $\tfrac12(1-1/p)\ge\tfrac13$ for odd $p$ and $\tfrac12$ for $p=2$, so $|\cT_q|\ge M_q/3-O(1)$, uniformly in $p$. Take $q$ so large that $q>8M_q$; then all multipliers and endpoints constructed below are positive.

\emph{Even prime powers.} Let $q=2^a\ge4$ and $t\in\cT_q$, so $t$ is odd. Let $1\le r<q$ be the inverse of $t$ modulo $q$, and put
\[
m_1=\frac{rt-1}q,\qquad m_2=t-m_1 .
\]
Since $rt\equiv1\pmod q$ and $rt>1$, we have $rt\ge q+1$, so $m_1\ge1$; since $r<q$, $m_1<t$, so $m_2\ge1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S5}
qm_1+1=rt,\qquad qm_2-1=(q-r)t,\qquad m_1+m_2=t .
\end{equation}
Both multipliers are positive and less than $2M_q$. Since $t$ is odd, exactly one of $m_1,m_2$ is odd. Choose that one as $m$, with $\sigma=+1$ if $m=m_1$ and $\sigma=-1$ if $m=m_2$; then $p=2\nmid m$. As $4\mid q$, the endpoint $qm$ is divisible by four, and we set $y=qm$.

\emph{Odd prime powers.} Let $p$ be odd and $t\in\cT_q$, so $\gcd(4t,q)=1$. Let $1\le r<q$ satisfy $4rt\equiv-1\pmod q$, and put
\[
m_1=\frac{4rt+1}q,\qquad m_2=4t-m_1 .
\]
Since $4rt+1\equiv0\pmod q$ and $4rt+1>1$, $m_1\ge1$; and $m_1=4t$ would force $4t(q-r)=1$, so $m_1<4t$ and $m_2\ge1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S6}
qm_1-1=4rt,\qquad qm_2+1=4(q-r)t,\qquad m_1+m_2=4t .
\end{equation}
Both multipliers are positive and less than $8M_q$. Since $p\nmid4t$, at least one of them is coprime to $p$. Choose such an $m$, with $\sigma=-1$ if $m=m_1$ and $\sigma=+1$ if $m=m_2$; fix a deterministic rule if both are admissible. The neighbour $qm+\sigma$ is $4rt$ or $4(q-r)t$, hence divisible by four, and we set $y=qm+\sigma$.

In both cases no inverse is required to lie in a prescribed subinterval of its residue range; the whole range $1\le r<q$ is used. Each $t\in\cT_q$ thus yields one signed multiplier $(m,\sigma)$; the neighbour has the form $ft$ or $4ft$ with $1\le f<q$, and is at most $8qM_q$.

\emph{First sieve: large prime factors of $t$.} Put
\[
\eta=\varepsilon/10000,\qquad T_q=q^{\varepsilon-\eta}.
\]
Discard every $t$ having a prime factor exceeding $T_q$. Counting multiples of each such prime in $[1,2M_q]$, their number is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S7}
2M_q\sum_{\substack{T_q<\ell\le2M_q\\ \ell\ \text{prime}}}\frac1\ell+\pi(2M_q)
=\Bigl(2\log\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon-\eta}+o(1)\Bigr)M_q<\frac{M_q}{1000}
\end{equation}
by \eqref{eq:mertens} and Chebyshev's bound.

\emph{Second sieve: large prime powers in the neighbour.} Suppose a prime power $u=\ell^b\ge q$ divides the neighbour $ft$ or $4ft$ of a surviving $t$. If $\ell\nmid4t$ then $u\mid f$, which is impossible because $f<q\le u$. Hence $\ell\mid4t$, so $\ell\le T_q$ (for large $q$, $T_q\ge2$); and $\ell\ne p$, because the neighbour is congruent to $\sigma\not\equiv0$ modulo $p$. There are $O_\varepsilon(T_q\log q)$ prime powers $u$ with $\ell\le T_q$ in the range $q\le u\le8qM_q+1$. For each such $u$ and each sign, the congruence $qm\equiv-\sigma\pmod u$ has at most one solution $m\in[1,8M_q]$, because $\gcd(q,u)=1$ and $8M_q<q\le u$. Each signed multiplier $(m,\sigma)$ arises from at most $V(q)$ parameters $t$, because $t$ divides the neighbour $qm+\sigma$, which lies in the range \eqref{eq:envelopecontain}. The second sieve therefore discards at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S8}
O_\varepsilon\bigl(T_qV(q)\log q\bigr)=o(M_q)
\end{equation}
parameters. For \eqref{eq:S8} it suffices that $V(q)\le q^{\eta/4}$ and $\log q\le q^{\eta/4}$, which hold for large $q$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:envelope}; then $T_qV(q)\log q\le q^{\varepsilon-\eta/2}$. This is a much stronger use of the divisor bound than the envelope estimate needed for the mass bound in Section~\ref{sec:descentprop}.

\emph{Third sieve: small multipliers.} Discard the parameters whose multiplier satisfies $m<R(q)$. Each $m$ has at most $2V(q)$ preimages $t$, allowing both signs, so this costs at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S9}
2(R(q)+1)V(q)=\frac{2M_q}{V(q)\log q}+2V(q)=o(M_q).
\end{equation}

\emph{Conclusion.} By \eqref{eq:S7}--\eqref{eq:S9}, at least $M_q/3-M_q/1000-o(M_q)\ge M_q/4$ parameters $t$ remain for large $q$. Since each signed multiplier has at most $2V(q)$ preimages, the surviving parameters produce at least $M_q/(8V(q))$ distinct multipliers $m$. Select one signed pair for each; this is $A_q$, and \eqref{eq:S2}--\eqref{eq:S4} hold by construction: the neighbour of a surviving $t$ has no prime-power divisor $u\ge q$, so it is strictly $q$-powersmooth.

\emph{Positions and labels.} In the even case distinct multipliers give distinct positions $qm$. In the odd case, $qm+\sigma=qm'+\sigma'$ with $m\ne m'$ would force $q\mid\sigma'-\sigma\in\{\pm2\}$, impossible for odd $q>2$. Since $m\le8M_q<q$ and $p\nmid m$, the largest prime power dividing $qm$ is exactly $q$; the neighbour is strictly $q$-powersmooth. Hence the label $q$ can be recovered from the unordered pair.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite simultaneous orientation}\label{sec:orient}

\begin{lemma}[compatible fibers]\label{lem:orient}
For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$ there is an integer $L_0\ge2$ with the following property. For every integer $L\ge L_0$ and every integer $H\ge L$ there are sets and signs
\[
(I_q,\sigma_q)_{L<q\le H,\ q\ \text{prime power}},\qquad I_q\subseteq A_q,\qquad |I_q|\ge q^{7\varepsilon/8},
\]
with $\sigma_q$ the signs of Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber}, such that the retained intervals $J(q,m,\sigma_q(m))$, over all $m\in I_q$ and all prime powers $L<q\le H$, are pairwise distinct and pairwise separated.
\end{lemma}

The statement is finite: it asserts a compatible family for the labels up to $H$, chosen after $H$ is known, and not a single compatible infinite family. The finite statement is exactly what the descent needs.

\begin{proof}
Let $N_q=|A_q|$. By \eqref{eq:S2} and \eqref{eq:envelopesize}, choose $L_0$ so large that for every prime power $q>L_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S10}
N_q\ge q^{15\varepsilon/16},\qquad N_q/4\ge q^{7\varepsilon/8},\qquad
(9/10)^{q^{15\varepsilon/16}}\le\frac1{2q^2}.
\end{equation}
Fix $L\ge L_0$ and $H\ge L$. If no prime power lies in $(L,H]$ there is nothing to prove, so assume there is one. Consider the finite set $S$ of all positions $y_q(m)$, $m\in A_q$, $L<q\le H$. To each $y\in S$ assign a bit, choosing either the left interval $[y-1,y]$ or the right interval $[y,y+1]$; there are $2^{|S|}$ assignments, and we average over all of them with equal weight. Retain a candidate $(q,m)$ when its interval $J(q,m,\sigma_q(m))$ agrees with the bit at its position.

Fix a label $q$. Its $N_q$ positions are distinct, so the retained count $B_q$ is a sum of $N_q$ independent bits, each retained with probability $\tfrac12$. Averaging $2^{-B_q}$ over the assignments and expanding the product gives
\[
\E\bigl[2^{-B_q}\bigr]=\Bigl(\tfrac12\cdot1+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12\Bigr)^{N_q}=(3/4)^{N_q},
\]
and therefore, since $2^{-B_q}>2^{-N_q/4}$ whenever $B_q<N_q/4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S12}
\Pr\bigl(B_q<N_q/4\bigr)\le2^{N_q/4}(3/4)^{N_q}<(9/10)^{N_q}\le\frac1{2q^2},
\end{equation}
using $2^{1/4}\cdot3/4<9/10$ and \eqref{eq:S10}. Here $\Pr$ and $\E$ are normalized counts over the finite cube of bit assignments. A union bound over the labels gives total failure weight at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S13}
\frac12\sum_{q=L+1}^H\frac1{q^2}\le\frac12\sum_{q=L+1}^H\frac1{q(q-1)}\le\frac1{2L}<1 .
\end{equation}
Hence some assignment retains at least $N_q/4\ge q^{7\varepsilon/8}$ candidates in every fiber; let $I_q$ be the retained multipliers.

\emph{Separation.} Consider two retained intervals at positions $y<z$. Both are multiples of four, so $z\ge y+4$. The earlier interval is contained in $[y-1,y+1]$ and ends at $b\le y+1$; the later starts at $a'\ge z-1\ge y+3$. Thus $b+1\le y+2<a'$, the required omitted integer. At a common position only one orientation is retained, and a retained interval at that position and with that orientation is a single interval; two different labels cannot supply the same interval because the largest prime power dividing its endpoints identifies the label (Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber}), and within one fiber positions are distinct. So retained intervals at a common position coincide, and distinct retained intervals have distinct positions and are separated.
\end{proof}

The bit selects left or right relative to the position $y$, not a uniform sign: for $p=2$ the position is $y=qm$ and $\sigma=+1$ means the right interval, while for odd $p$ the position is $y=qm+\sigma$ and $\sigma=+1$ means the left interval $[y-1,y]$. Retained left endpoints may differ by as little as three; separation, not a gap of four, is the conclusion.

\section{Sparse modular inverse subsets cover all residues}\label{sec:cover}

\subsection{A pigeonhole lemma}\label{sec:pigeon}

\begin{proposition}[simultaneous approximation]\label{prop:pigeon}
Let $q\ge2$ and $d\ge1$, let $d_1,\ldots,d_d$ be integers, and let $a_1,\ldots,a_d\ge1$ be real numbers with $\Vgap=\prod_ia_i<q$. Put
\[
B_i=\frac{4q}{a_i}\Bigl(\frac{\Vgap}q\Bigr)^{1/d}.
\]
Then there are $1\le T<q$ and integers $e_i\equiv Td_i\pmod q$ with $|e_i|\le B_i$ for all $i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Consider the $q$ vectors $(td_i\bmod q)_{1\le i\le d}$, $0\le t<q$, in $[0,q)^d$. Ignore the coordinates with $B_i\ge q$, and partition each remaining coordinate range $[0,q)$ into intervals of length $B_i$, at most $2q/B_i$ of them. Note that $\prod_i(q/B_i)=q/4^d$, and that $B_i\le4q$ for every $i$ because $\Vgap<q$ and $a_i\ge1$. If $r\ge1$ coordinates remain, the number of boxes is at most
\[
\prod_{i:\,B_i<q}\frac{2q}{B_i}\le2^r\cdot\frac{q}{4^d}\cdot4^{d-r}=q/2^r<q .
\]
If no coordinate remains there is one box. In either case two of the $q$ vectors share a box. Their difference, taken with positive sign, has the form $(Td_i\bmod q)_i$ with $1\le T<q$, and each coordinate is congruent to an integer of absolute value at most $B_i$; ignored coordinates impose no restriction because $B_i\ge q$ there.
\end{proof}

The corresponding step in \cite{CFHMPSV} is formulated with a coprimality hypothesis on the generators $d_i$. That hypothesis is not available in the setting of Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}, where only the elements of $J$, not the generators of $P$, are known to be units modulo $q$; the pigeonhole argument above does not need it.

\subsection{The covering lemma}\label{sec:coverlemma}

\begin{lemma}[sparse inverse covering]\label{lem:cover}
For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$ and $C\ge1$ there is $q_0(\varepsilon,C)$ such that, for every prime power $q\ge q_0$ and every finite set
\[
I\subseteq[1,Cq^\varepsilon]\cap\N,\qquad \gcd(i,q)=1\ \ (i\in I),\qquad |I|\ge q^{7\varepsilon/8},
\]
every residue modulo $q$ is a sum of inverses of at most
\[
s=\lfloor q^{\varepsilon/2}\rfloor
\]
distinct members of $I$.
\end{lemma}

This is a sparse extension of \cite[Theorem 2]{CFHMPSV}. The proof follows their progression and divisor-count argument, with the density loss accounted for explicitly. The descent uses the lemma with $C=8$; the threshold $q_0$ depends on $C$.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Structure theorem.} Take $q$ so large that $Cq^\varepsilon<q$. Then reduction modulo $q$, and hence inversion, is injective on $I$. Represent the inverses of the members of $I$ by integers in $[1,q-1]$; let $A$ be the set of these representatives, of size $m=|I|$, so that
\[
q^{7\varepsilon/8}\le m\le Cq^\varepsilon .
\]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:CF} with $\beta=2/\varepsilon$, $\eta=1/3$ and ambient parameter $n=q$. For sufficiently large $q$,
\[
m\ge m_0,\qquad q\le m^{2/\varepsilon},\qquad m^{1/3}\le s\le\frac{cm}{\log_2m},
\]
so we obtain $P$, $J$ and $J'$. The structural loss $s\log_2m/c$ is $o(m)$, so for large $q$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G1}
|J|\ge\tfrac34m .
\end{equation}
Keep the representation defining the theorem's dilate fixed:
\[
P=\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{D}n_id_i:\ \alpha_i\le n_i\le\beta_i,\ n_i\in\Z\Bigr\},\qquad D\le d_0 .
\]
Choose an integer coordinate vector $v$ representing $0\in P$. Put $\ell_i=\lceil\alpha_i\rceil$ and $u_i=\lfloor\beta_i\rfloor$. Call a coordinate \emph{active} when $u_i>\ell_i$; all other coordinates are constant on $P$. Relabel the active coordinates $1,\ldots,d$ and put
\[
a_i=u_i-\ell_i\ge1,\qquad \Vgap=\prod_{i=1}^da_i .
\]
Every $j\in J$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jrep}
j=\sum_{i=1}^d(n_i-v_i)d_i,\qquad |n_i-v_i|\le a_i,
\end{equation}
since the contributions of the constant coordinates vanish after subtracting the representation of zero. There is at least one active coordinate, because $P$ contains zero and the nonzero elements of $J$.

\emph{Upper bound for $\Vgap$.} The dilate $csP$ is nonempty by Theorem~\ref{thm:CF}. Fix each inactive coordinate at an integer of its dilated interval, which exists by nonemptiness. This produces a face of that same proper dilate; no representation is changed and no new dilation is formed. For an active coordinate, its dilated interval contains at least
\[
cs(\beta_i-\alpha_i)-1\ge csa_i-1\ge(cs/2)a_i
\]
integers for large $q$. Thus the face has at least $(cs/2)^d\Vgap$ points. Its supplied translate lies in the subset sums of $J'$, which lie in $[0,sq]$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G2}
(cs/2)^d\Vgap\le sq+1,\qquad \Vgap\ll_\varepsilon qs^{1-d}\ll_\varepsilon q^{1-(d-1)\varepsilon/2}.
\end{equation}
If $\Vgap\ge q$ this already forces $d=1$, and the final paragraph below applies. Assume $\Vgap<q$.

\emph{Lower bound for $\Vgap$.} Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:pigeon} to the active generators and the numbers $a_i$, obtaining $1\le T<q$ and $e_i\equiv Td_i\pmod q$ with $|e_i|\le B_i$. Put $x=(\Vgap/q)^{1/d}$. For each $j\in J$, by \eqref{eq:jrep} the centered representative $h$ of $Tj\bmod q$ satisfies
\[
|h|\le\min\Bigl\{\frac q2,\ \sum_ia_iB_i\Bigr\}=\min\{q/2,\ 4dqx\}.
\]
Pair this $h$ with the $i\in I$ for which $j\equiv i^{-1}\pmod q$; distinct $j$ give distinct $i$, so there are at least $|J|$ distinct ordered pairs $(i,h)$, whether or not multiplication by $T$ is injective on $J$. They satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G3}
ih=qz+T,\qquad z\in\Z,
\end{equation}
and the product is nonzero because $0<T<q$. Since $i\le Cq^\varepsilon$,
\[
|ih|\le\tfrac C2q^{1+\varepsilon},\qquad |ih|\le4Cdq^{1+\varepsilon}x .
\]
The second bound and \eqref{eq:G3} confine $z$ to a real interval of length $8Cdq^\varepsilon x$, so $z$ has at most $1+8Cdq^\varepsilon x$ integer possibilities. Define the divisor envelope
\[
\Delta_C(q)=\max_{1\le n\le\lceil Cq^{1+\varepsilon}\rceil}\tau(n)=q^{o(1)}
\]
(Proposition~\ref{prop:envelope}), and take $q$ large enough that $\Delta_C(q)\le m/4$. For each $z$, the positive integer $i$ divides the nonzero integer $|qz+T|\le\lceil Cq^{1+\varepsilon}\rceil$ and determines $h$, so there are at most $\Delta_C(q)$ ordered pairs $(i,h)$ with that $z$. Therefore
\[
\tfrac34m\le|J|\le\Delta_C(q)\bigl(1+8Cdq^\varepsilon x\bigr)\le\tfrac14m+8Cd\,\Delta_C(q)\,q^\varepsilon x,
\]
so $x\ge m/(16Cd\,\Delta_C(q)\,q^\varepsilon)$ and, using $m\ge q^{7\varepsilon/8}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G4}
\Vgap=qx^d\ge\frac{q\,m^d}{(16Cd)^d\Delta_C(q)^dq^{d\varepsilon}}\ge\frac{q^{1-d\varepsilon/8}}{(16Cd)^d\Delta_C(q)^d}.
\end{equation}
Because $C$ is fixed and $d\le d_0$ is bounded, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Vlower}
\Vgap\ge q^{1-d\varepsilon/8-o(1)}.
\end{equation}
For $d\ge2$ this contradicts \eqref{eq:G2}, because
\[
\frac{(d-1)\varepsilon}2-\frac{d\varepsilon}8=\frac{(3d-4)\varepsilon}8>0 .
\]
The rank is bounded independently of $q$, so these comparisons are uniform. Thus $d=1$.

\emph{Conclusion.} By \eqref{eq:jrep} every $j\in J$ is a multiple of $d_1$. Since these $j$ are units modulo $q$, $\gcd(d_1,q)=1$. The translated one-dimensional face of the dilate contained in the subset sums of $J'$ has at least $(c/2)s\Vgap$ terms. If $\Vgap\ge q$ this exceeds $q$ for large $q$; otherwise \eqref{eq:Vlower} gives
\[
(c/2)s\Vgap\ge(c/2)q^{1+3\varepsilon/8-o(1)}>q .
\]
An arithmetic progression with common difference coprime to $q$ and more than $q$ terms covers every residue modulo $q$. Every such term is a subset sum of $J'$, which has at most $s$ members, that is, a sum of at most $s$ inverses of distinct members of $I$.
\end{proof}

\section{The correction and descent mechanism}\label{sec:descent}

From now on $\varepsilon=1/10$, and $L$, $K$, $\delta$ are as in Section~\ref{sec:params}.

\subsection{Powersmooth supply}\label{sec:supply}

\begin{lemma}[powersmooth supply]\label{lem:supply}
Let $0<\theta<1$ and $0<a<b$ be fixed, let $x\to\infty$, and let $y=x^{\theta+o(1)}$. The number of integers in $[ax,bx]$ that are not $y$-powersmooth is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supply}
\bigl((b-a)\log(1/\theta)+o(1)\bigr)x .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The number of integers in $[ax,bx]$ divisible by some prime $p>y$ is at most
\[
(b-a)x\sum_{y<p\le bx}\frac1p+O(\pi(bx))=\bigl((b-a)\log(1/\theta)+o(1)\bigr)x
\]
by \eqref{eq:mertens} and $\pi(bx)=o(x)$. For each prime $p\le y$ let $u_p$ be the smallest power of $p$ exceeding $y$. The number of integers at most $bx$ divisible by some $u_p$ is at most
\[
\sum_{p\le y}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{bx}{u_p}\Bigr\rfloor\le bx\frac{\pi(y)}y=o(x).
\]
Every integer that is not $y$-powersmooth is counted by one of the two bounds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Possible correction endpoints}\label{sec:Pstar}

Define the deterministic set of \emph{possible signed correction endpoints}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D1}
\cP^*_L=\bigcup_{\substack{q>L\\ q\ \text{prime power}}}\ \bigcup_{\substack{m\in\N\\ R(q)\le m\le8q^\varepsilon}}\{qm-1,\ qm,\ qm+1\}.
\end{equation}
It contains both endpoints of every possible signed correction pair $J(q,m,\sigma)$ with $m\in A_q$, before any orientation is chosen, and it depends neither on $H$, nor on the bit assignment of Lemma~\ref{lem:orient}, nor on the subsets used in cancellation.

\begin{proposition}[density zero]\label{prop:density}
For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$ and every cutoff $L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D2}
|\cP^*_L\cap[1,X]|=o(X)\qquad(X\to\infty).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The stronger rate $O(X/\log X)$, which held for the narrower endpoint set of the earlier construction, is not claimed for this enlarged set; every later use needs only density zero.

\begin{proof}
Fix $0<\zeta<\varepsilon$. By \eqref{eq:envelopesize} there is $q_1$ with $E(q)\le q^\zeta$ for $q\ge q_1$; the finitely many labels $q<q_1$ contribute only finitely many endpoints. For a label $q\ge q_1$, every endpoint is at least $qm-1\ge qR(q)-1\ge q^{1+\varepsilon-\zeta}-1$, so an endpoint at most $X$ forces
\[
q^{1+\varepsilon-\zeta}\le qm\le X+1,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad q\le Z:=(X+1)^{1/(1+\varepsilon-\zeta)} .
\]
Put $Y=X^{1/(1+\varepsilon)}$. Labels $q\le Y$ contribute at most
\[
3\sum_{\substack{q\le Y\\ q\ \text{prime power}}}8q^\varepsilon=O\Bigl(\frac{Y^{1+\varepsilon}}{\log Y}\Bigr)=O\Bigl(\frac X{\log X}\Bigr)
\]
endpoints, by Chebyshev's bound. For $Y<q\le Z$ the multipliers with $qm\le X+1$ number at most $(X+1)/q+1$, so these labels contribute at most
\[
3(X+1)\sum_{\substack{Y<q\le Z\\ q\ \text{prime power}}}\frac1q+3Z .
\]
For prime labels, \eqref{eq:mertens} gives
\[
\sum_{Y<p\le Z}\frac1p=\log\frac{\log Z}{\log Y}+o(1)=\log\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon-\zeta}+o(1).
\]
Higher prime powers contribute $o(1)$: indeed
\[
\sum_{\substack{p^a>Y\\ a\ge2}}p^{-a}=O(Y^{-1/2}),
\]
since for $p\le\sqrt Y$ the geometric tail of the powers exceeding $Y$ is at most $2/Y$, and for $p>\sqrt Y$ the tails are bounded by $2\sum_{n>\sqrt Y}n^{-2}$. Finally $Z=o(X)$ because $\zeta<\varepsilon$. Consequently
\[
\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{|\cP^*_L\cap[1,X]|}X\le3\log\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon-\zeta}.
\]
The order of limits is: fix $\zeta$, let $X\to\infty$, then let $\zeta\downarrow0$. Equivalently, given $\kappa>0$, first choose $\zeta$ so that the displayed constant is less than $\kappa/2$, then choose $X$ large. This proves \eqref{eq:D2}.
\end{proof}

Since $\cP^*_L\subseteq\cP^*_{L_{\rm base}}$ for $L\ge L_{\rm base}$, the density statement for the fixed set $\cP^*_{L_{\rm base}}$ bounds every later $\cP^*_L$ uniformly, as used in Section~\ref{sec:params}.

\subsection{Auxiliary pairs}\label{sec:aux}

\begin{lemma}[auxiliary pairs]\label{lem:aux}
For every sufficiently large $L$ one can fix, simultaneously for each prime power $q>L$, a family $\cF_q$ of exactly
\[
s(q)=\lfloor q^{1/20}\rfloor
\]
pairs $[a,a+1]$ satisfying
\[
q^{11/10}\le a,\qquad a+1\le2q^{11/10},\qquad 4\mid a,
\]
with both endpoints $(q/2)$-powersmooth, such that all auxiliary pairs, over all $q>L$, are pairwise separated, and every auxiliary endpoint has distance at least two from every element of $\cP^*_L$. In particular every auxiliary pair is separated from every possible signed correction pair. If $\cF^*$ is the set of all auxiliary endpoints, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fstar}
|\cF^*\cap[1,X]|=O(X^{21/22}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Construct the families in increasing order of the numerical prime power $q$. Put $x=q^{11/10}$ and $y=q/2$, so $\theta=10/11$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:supply}. The candidate pairs $[a,a+1]$ with $4\mid a$ contained in $[x,2x]$ number $x/4+O(1)$; they are pairwise separated. Each integer that is not $y$-powersmooth spoils at most one candidate, so at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auxcount}
\Bigl(\frac14-\log\frac{11}{10}+o(1)\Bigr)x
\end{equation}
candidates have both endpoints $y$-powersmooth; the constant is positive.

Delete every candidate having an endpoint within distance one of $\cP^*_L$. Each element of $\cP^*_L\cap[x-2,2x+2]$ spoils at most four candidates, so by Proposition~\ref{prop:density} this deletes only $o(x)$ candidates. Delete also every candidate having an endpoint within distance one of a previously chosen auxiliary endpoint. The number deleted in this second step is at most a constant times
\[
\sum_{\substack{q'<q\\ q'\ \text{prime power}}}s(q')\le q^{21/20}=o(q^{11/10})=o(x).
\]
Thus a positive constant times $x$ candidates remain, more than $s(q)$ for all sufficiently large $q$. Select any $s(q)$ of them as $\cF_q$. All estimates are uniform in $q$, so a single sufficiently large $L$ suffices for the entire ascending construction. Separation between auxiliary pairs of the same $q$ holds because all candidates are separated; between different $q$ it holds by the second deletion. Every auxiliary endpoint has distance at least two from $\cP^*_L$ by the first deletion, and a possible signed correction pair has both endpoints in $\cP^*_L$, so it is separated from every auxiliary pair.

For \eqref{eq:Fstar}, an auxiliary endpoint at most $X$ has label $q\le X^{10/11}$, so
\[
|\cF^*\cap[1,X]|\le2\sum_{\substack{q\le X^{10/11}\\ q\ \text{prime power}}}q^{1/20}=O(X^{21/22}).
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The families $\cF_q$ depend only on $L$; they are fixed once and for all, independently of $k$ and of every later finite choice of compatible fibers. Put $\cU=\cP^*_L\cup\cF^*$. By \eqref{eq:D2} and \eqref{eq:Fstar},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D3}
|\cU\cap[1,X]|=o(X).
\end{equation}

\subsection{The signed cancellation identity}\label{sec:cancel}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:cancel}
For a signed correction pair $J(q,m,\sigma)$ with $m\in A_q$, in the ring of rationals whose denominators are coprime to $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D4}
q\,w_\sigma(qm)=\frac1m+\frac q{qm+\sigma}\equiv m^{-1}\pmod q,
\end{equation}
because $p\nmid m$ and $qm+\sigma\equiv\sigma\not\equiv0\pmod p$, so both denominators are coprime to $p$. Strict $q$-powersmoothness of $qm+\sigma$ is a separate matter: it guarantees that the denominator of the second term introduces only prime powers below $q$. This is the identity behind the descent: subtracting pair masses whose $m^{-1}$ sum to the right residue removes the factor $q$ from the denominator. The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:descent} makes this explicit with a common denominator.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The descent}\label{sec:descentprop}

\begin{proposition}[signed descent]\label{prop:descent}
Let $L$, $K$, $\delta$ and the families $\cF_q$ be as in Section~\ref{sec:params}, let $H>L$ be an integer, and let $(I_q,\sigma_q)_{L<q\le H}$ be compatible fibers for this $H$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:orient}. Let $r_0$ be a rational number with $r_0\ge\delta/4$ whose reduced denominator has no prime-power divisor exceeding $H$. Then there is a set $\cC$ of exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CH}
C_H=\sum_{\substack{L<q\le H\\ q\ \text{prime power}}}s(q)\le H^{21/20}
\end{equation}
pairs, each either a retained signed correction pair $J(q,m,\sigma_q(m))$ with $m\in I_q$ or a member of $\cF_q$, for some prime power $q\in(L,H]$, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the pairs in $\cC$ are pairwise separated;
\item[(ii)] every endpoint of a pair in $\cC$ lies in $\cU$, is at least $L$, and is at most $8H^{11/10}+1$;
\item[(iii)] the mass $W_{\cC}$ of $\cC$ satisfies $W_{\cC}\le120L^{-1/40}<\delta/16$;
\item[(iv)] $r=r_0-W_{\cC}$ satisfies $r\ge r_0-\delta/16>0$, and its reduced denominator divides $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Visit every prime power $q\in(L,H]$ in decreasing numerical order, maintaining a current deficit, initially $r_0$. The invariant before stage $q$ is that no prime power larger than $q$ divides the current reduced denominator; it holds initially by hypothesis.

\emph{Stage $q=p^a$.} If $q$ is the full power of $p$ in the current denominator, write the current deficit as $u/(qv)$ with $p\nmid uv$. The retained fiber $I_q$ satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} with $C=8$: its members are coprime to $q$ and at most $8q^\varepsilon$, and $|I_q|\ge q^{7\varepsilon/8}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} therefore selects $S\subseteq I_q$ with $c_q=|S|\le s(q)$ such that
\[
\sum_{m\in S}m^{-1}\equiv uv^{-1}\pmod q .
\]
Write $\sigma_m=\sigma_q(m)$ and subtract the masses $w_{\sigma_m}(qm)$, $m\in S$, from the deficit. To see the effect, let
\[
D=\lcm\bigl(v,\ \{m:m\in S\},\ \{qm+\sigma_m:m\in S\}\bigr).
\]
All these integers are coprime to $p$: $p\nmid v$, $p\nmid m$, and $qm+\sigma_m\equiv\sigma_m\not\equiv0\pmod p$. The exact calculation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D5}
qD\Bigl(\frac u{qv}-\sum_{m\in S}w_{\sigma_m}(qm)\Bigr)=\frac{uD}v-\sum_{m\in S}\frac Dm-q\sum_{m\in S}\frac D{qm+\sigma_m},
\end{equation}
an integer divisible by $q$ by the chosen congruence. The corrected deficit therefore has the form $z/D$ with $z\in\Z$, so its reduced denominator is coprime to $p$: the full current $p$-power has been eliminated. Every newly introduced prime power is smaller than $q$: those from $m$ because $m<q$, and those from $qm+\sigma_m$ by \eqref{eq:S3}. Strict descent below $q$ is all that is needed; no bound $q/2$ is claimed for correction neighbours. If the current denominator has no prime-power factor numerically equal to $q$, set $S=\varnothing$ and $c_q=0$.

In either case, subtract next the masses of any $s(q)-c_q$ pairs from $\cF_q$. Their denominators introduce only prime powers at most $q/2$. They may reintroduce a smaller power of the same prime $p$, which is handled at its later turn. After the complete stage every prime power in the denominator is strictly smaller than $q$, which is the invariant for the next stage, including at stages using only auxiliary pairs. Every prime power in $(L,H]$ is visited, including those at which only padding is needed.

\emph{Count and endpoints.} Each stage adds exactly $s(q)$ pairs, so $|\cC|=C_H$, and $C_H\le\sum_{n\le H}n^{1/20}\le H^{21/20}$. A retained correction pair has endpoints in $\{qm-1,qm,qm+1\}$ with $m\le8q^\varepsilon$, so they are at most $8q^{11/10}+1\le8H^{11/10}+1$, and at least $qm-1\ge q-1\ge L$; an auxiliary pair has endpoints in $[q^{11/10},2q^{11/10}]$, which lie in the same range. Correction endpoints lie in $\cP^*_L$ and auxiliary endpoints in $\cF^*$, so all endpoints lie in $\cU$.

\emph{Separation.} Retained correction pairs are pairwise separated by Lemma~\ref{lem:orient}; auxiliary pairs are pairwise separated and separated from every possible correction pair by Lemma~\ref{lem:aux}.

\emph{Mass.} For $qm\ge2$,
\[
w_{-1}(qm)=\frac1{qm}+\frac1{qm-1}\le\frac3{qm},\qquad w_{+1}(qm)\le\frac2{qm}\le\frac3{qm}.
\]
With $\varepsilon=1/10$, $m\ge R(q)=q^{1/10}/E(q)$ and $E(q)\le q^{1/40}$ (condition (f) of Section~\ref{sec:params}),
\[
w_{\sigma_m}(qm)\le3E(q)q^{-11/10}.
\]
Each auxiliary pair has mass at most $2q^{-11/10}\le3E(q)q^{-11/10}$, since $E(q)\ge1$. A stage of exactly $s(q)$ pairs therefore costs at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D6}
3s(q)E(q)q^{-11/10}\le3q^{1/20+1/40-11/10}=3q^{-41/40}.
\end{equation}
Summing over all integers $q>L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D7}
W_{\cC}\le3\sum_{n>L}n^{-41/40}\le3\int_L^\infty x^{-41/40}\,dx=120L^{-1/40}<\frac\delta{16}
\end{equation}
by condition (h) of Section~\ref{sec:params}.

\emph{Residual.} On completion the reduced denominator has only prime powers at most $L$, each of which divides $K=\lcm(1,\ldots,L)$; hence the reduced denominator of $r$ divides $K$, and $r\ge r_0-W_{\cC}>r_0-\delta/16\ge\delta/4-\delta/16>0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The core}\label{sec:core}

\subsection{Dense bands contain a unit-fraction sum}\label{sec:dense}

\begin{lemma}[dense bands]\label{lem:dense}
If $T\to\infty$ through positive integers and $E_T\subseteq[T,4T)\cap\Z$ has size $o(T)$, then $[T,4T)\setminus E_T$ has a subset whose reciprocals sum to $1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The density of $[T,4T)\setminus E_T$ in $[1,4T]$ tends to $3/4>1-1/e+1/20$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:LS} with $\zeta=1/20$ and $N=4T$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Core pairs and their extensions}\label{sec:coreconstr}

For $k\to\infty$ put, as in Section~\ref{sec:params},
\[
Q=\lfloor k^{4/5}\rfloor,\qquad H=KBQ+2,
\]
and define the set of \emph{reserved parameters}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reserved}
\cR=\bigl\{d\in[Q,BQ)\cap\Z:\ [Kd-1,Kd+3]\cap\cU=\varnothing\bigr\},\qquad R=|\cR| .
\end{equation}
The protected window $[Kd-1,Kd+3]$ contains the one-integer neighbourhood of the possible core triple $[Kd,Kd+2]$. Thus neither that triple nor its shorter pair $[Kd+1,Kd+2]$ can overlap or be adjacent to any pair with both endpoints in $\cU$, in particular to any pair in the set $\cC$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}, whichever orientations and subsets that proposition uses. The core is protected against the deterministic superset $\cP^*_L$ of every possible signed endpoint, not only against a previously selected family.

\begin{proposition}[core]\label{prop:core}
For sufficiently large $k$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $R=(B-1)Q-o(Q)$; in particular $R=o(k)$, and $C_H=O(k^{21/25})=o(k)$.
\item[(ii)] The core pairs $[Kd+1,Kd+2]$, $d\in\cR$, are pairwise separated, as are the triples $[Kd,Kd+2]$, $d\in\cR$, and every endpoint is at most $H$. Their mass satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wcore}
W_{\core}\le\frac2K\sum_{Q\le d<BQ}\frac1d=\frac{2j_0\log4}K+o(1)\le4\delta\log4+o(1)<\frac18 .
\end{equation}
\item[(iii)] There are pairwise disjoint sets $D_0,\ldots,D_{j_0-1}\subseteq\cR$ with $\sum_{d\in D_i}1/d=1$ for each $i$. Consequently, for every integer $1\le j\le j_0$, replacing the core pair $[Kd+1,Kd+2]$ by the triple $[Kd,Kd+2]$ for every $d\in D_0\cup\cdots\cup D_{j-1}$ adds exactly $j/K$ to the mass and changes neither the number $R$ of core intervals nor their separation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Every excluded $d\in[Q,BQ)$ has a witness $n\in\cU\cap[Kd-1,Kd+3]$, and these windows are disjoint for distinct $d$ because $K\ge8$; all witnesses lie in $[1,KBQ+3]$. Hence the number of excluded parameters is at most $|\cU\cap[1,KBQ+3]|=o(KBQ)=o(Q)$ by \eqref{eq:D3}, since $K$ and $B$ are fixed. So $R=(B-1)Q-o(Q)$, which is $\Theta(k^{4/5})=o(k)$. By \eqref{eq:CH}, $C_H\le H^{21/20}=O(Q^{21/20})=O(k^{21/25})$.

(ii) Distinct triples $[Kd,Kd+2]$ have starts differing by at least $K\ge8$, so they are separated, and so are the pairs they contain. All endpoints are at most $KBQ+2=H$. The mass bound \eqref{eq:Wcore} uses $\sum_{Q\le d<BQ}1/d=\log B+o(1)=j_0\log4+o(1)$ and $j_0/K\le\delta+1/K\le2\delta$ by condition (i) of Section~\ref{sec:params}.

(iii) Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:dense} in the $j_0$ disjoint bands
\[
[4^iQ,4^{i+1}Q),\qquad 0\le i<j_0,
\]
all of which lie inside $[Q,BQ)$ because $B=4^{j_0}$. Each band loses only the $o(Q)=o(4^iQ)$ parameters excluded from $\cR$, and $j_0$ is fixed, so Lemma~\ref{lem:dense} with $T=4^iQ$ gives $D_i\subseteq\cR\cap[4^iQ,4^{i+1}Q)$ with $\sum_{d\in D_i}1/d=1$. The sets $D_i$ are disjoint because the bands are. Extending $[Kd+1,Kd+2]$ to $[Kd,Kd+2]$ adds $1/(Kd)$ to the mass, so extending over $D_0\cup\cdots\cup D_{j-1}$ adds exactly $j/K$. The extended family is still separated by (ii), and its cardinality is still $R$.
\end{proof}

\section{Main pairs and the exact interval count}\label{sec:main}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:supply} with $y=Q=k^{4/5+o(1)}$ and $\theta=4/5$, in any fixed interval $[ak,bk]$ at most
\[
\bigl((b-a)\log(5/4)+o(1)\bigr)k
\]
integers are not $Q$-powersmooth. Consider the \emph{grid pairs} $[a,a+1]$ with $3\mid a$. Distinct grid pairs are separated, and an integer that is not $Q$-powersmooth spoils at most one of them. Thus $[ak,bk]$ contains at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
\gamma(b-a)k+o(k)\ \text{grid pairs with both endpoints $Q$-powersmooth},\qquad
\gamma=\frac13-\log\frac54>0,
\end{equation}
where only $O(1)$ pairs are affected by the boundary. Numerically $\gamma=0.110\ldots$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gammabounds}
10\gamma>1,\qquad16\gamma>1 .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[main pairs]\label{prop:main}
Let $M=k-R-C_H$ and $\tau_k=1-W_{\core}-\delta/2$. For sufficiently large $k$ there is a set $\cM$ of exactly $M$ grid pairs with both endpoints $Q$-powersmooth, contained in $[k/1024,k]\cup[4k,20k]$, pairwise separated, whose mass $W_{\main}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wmain}
\tau_k-2048/k\le W_{\main}\le\tau_k .
\end{equation}
Consequently the initial deficit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r0}
r_0=1-W_{\core}-W_{\main}\in[\delta/2,\ \delta/2+2048/k]
\end{equation}
is a rational number whose reduced denominator has no prime-power divisor exceeding $H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:core}(i), $M=k-o(k)$ is a positive integer for large $k$. Let $\cA$ be the set of all grid pairs with both endpoints $Q$-powersmooth contained in $[k/1024,k]$. Divide this interval into the ten dyadic bands $[t,2t]$, $t=2^{-i}k$, $1\le i\le10$. By \eqref{eq:gamma} each band contributes at least $\gamma t+o(k)$ pairs, each of mass at least $1/t$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Amass}
\sum_{A\in\cA}\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n\ge10\gamma+o(1)>1
\end{equation}
by \eqref{eq:gammabounds}. There are fewer than $k/3$ pairs in $\cA$, hence fewer than $M$.

The far band $[4k,20k]$ contains more than $k$ grid pairs with both endpoints $Q$-powersmooth, by \eqref{eq:gamma} and $16\gamma>1$. Choose any $M$ of them. Their total mass is at most $M/(2k)<1/2$.

Now replace far pairs, one at a time, by the pairs of $\cA$, until every pair of $\cA$ has been inserted. Each replacement removes a pair of mass less than $1/(2k)$ and inserts one of mass at most $2048/k$ and at least $2/(k+1)$, so it increases the mass by at most $2048/k$. By \eqref{eq:Amass} the mass at the end exceeds $1$. Since $W_{\core}<1/8$ by \eqref{eq:Wcore}, the target $\tau_k$ lies in $(1/2,1)$ for large $k$. Stop at the last configuration before the mass first exceeds $\tau_k$; this gives \eqref{eq:Wmain}. The count is exactly $M$ throughout. The pairs are separated within each band by the grid, and the two bands are separated from one another.

Finally, \eqref{eq:r0} follows from \eqref{eq:Wmain}. Every main endpoint is $Q$-powersmooth, and every core endpoint is at most $H$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:core}(ii), so all prime powers in the reduced denominator of $r_0$ are at most $H$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the main theorem}\label{sec:proof}

Let $k$ be sufficiently large for Propositions~\ref{prop:core} and \ref{prop:main} and for the estimates below, and put $Q=\lfloor k^{4/5}\rfloor$, $H=KBQ+2$ and $C_H$ as in \eqref{eq:CH}. Construct, in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the core pairs $[Kd+1,Kd+2]$, $d\in\cR$, and the sets $D_0,\ldots,D_{j_0-1}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:core}; these depend only on $\cU$, not on any orientation choice;
\item the compatible fibers $(I_q,\sigma_q)_{L<q\le H}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:orient} for this $H$, which is finite; condition (b) of Section~\ref{sec:params} gives $L\ge L_0$;
\item the $M=k-R-C_H$ main pairs $\cM$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:main}, with the initial deficit $r_0$ of \eqref{eq:r0};
\item the $C_H$ correction and auxiliary pairs $\cC$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}, applied to $r_0$, $H$ and the fibers of step 2. The hypotheses hold: $H>L$ for large $k$, $r_0\ge\delta/2\ge\delta/4$, and the denominator of $r_0$ has prime powers at most $H$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:main}.
\end{enumerate}
The residual $r=r_0-W_{\cC}$ satisfies, by Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}(iii)--(iv) and \eqref{eq:r0},
\[
\frac\delta4<\frac\delta2-\frac\delta{16}\le r\le\frac\delta2+\frac{2048}k\le\delta
\]
for large $k$, and its reduced denominator divides $K$. Hence $r=j/K$ with an integer $1\le j\le K\delta\le j_0$. Extend the core pairs indexed by $D_0\cup\cdots\cup D_{j-1}$ to triples, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:core}(iii). The total mass is now
\[
W_{\core}+W_{\main}+W_{\cC}+\frac jK=(1-r_0)+W_{\cC}+r=1 .
\]

\emph{Count.} The family consists of $R$ core intervals, $M$ main pairs and $C_H$ pairs from $\cC$; no interval was split or merged, so the total number is $R+M+C_H=k$. Every interval has two or three members.

\emph{Separation.} The pairs of $\cC$ are pairwise separated by Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}(i). Their endpoints lie in $\cU$, so by the definition \eqref{eq:reserved} of $\cR$ every core interval, extended or not, is separated from every pair of $\cC$, for both orientations and both signs of the correction pairs. Distinct core intervals are separated by Proposition~\ref{prop:core}(ii). Main pairs are separated from one another by Proposition~\ref{prop:main}. Finally, all endpoints of $\cC$ are at most
\[
8H^{11/10}+1=O(k^{22/25})=o(k),
\]
and all core endpoints are at most $H=O(k^{4/5})=o(k)$, so for large $k$ all of them lie below $k/1024-2$, separating them from every main pair. Thus the whole family is separated.

\emph{Endpoints.} Main endpoints are at most $20k$ and all other endpoints are $o(k)$, so $b_i\le20k$. Every left endpoint is at least $\min\{L,KQ,k/1024\}\ge2$: the endpoints of $\cC$ are at least $L$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}(ii), core endpoints are at least $KQ$, and main endpoints are at least $k/1024$.

Listing the intervals in increasing order gives exactly the conditions \eqref{eq:main}. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \qed

structure FamilyWitness (k : Nat) where
  intervals : Fin k -> NatInterval
  positive : forall i, 1 <= (intervals i).lo
  short : forall i, (intervals i).hi = (intervals i).lo + 1 \/
    (intervals i).hi = (intervals i).lo + 2
  separated : forall i j, i < j -> (intervals i).Separated (intervals j)
  bounded : forall i, (intervals i).hi <= 20 * k
  total_mass : sum i, (intervals i).mass = 1

def CandidateStatement : Prop :=
  exists k0 : Nat, forall k : Nat, k0 <= k -> Nonempty (FamilyWitness k)
\end{verbatim}

Here \texttt{NatInterval} is a pair of natural-number endpoints \texttt{lo}, \texttt{hi}; its carrier is the set of integers from \texttt{lo} to \texttt{hi} inclusive; \texttt{I.Separated J} means \texttt{I.hi + 1 < J.lo}; and \texttt{I.mass} is the exact rational sum of the reciprocals of the carrier. The field \texttt{positive} excludes the denominator zero; \texttt{short} gives exactly two or three members; \texttt{separated} with indices in \texttt{Fin k} gives exactly $k$ pairwise separated intervals; \texttt{total\_mass} is an exact equality in $\Q$. This is Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with the lower endpoint condition $a_i\ge1$ in place of $a_i\ge2$; Remark~\ref{rem:two} shows the two are equivalent given the other conditions. The intervals are indexed by $\{0,\ldots,k-1\}$ and are separated for every ordered pair of indices, which after sorting is the condition $b_i+1<a_{i+1}$.

\subsection{Correspondence between the paper and the formalization}\label{app:corr}

The kernel establishes the formal implications. The remaining human task is to check that the formal proposition says what the problem and this paper claim (Appendix~\ref{app:target}), and, for readers comparing the two proofs, that the formal components correspond to the components here. The following points were checked for that correspondence.

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.4cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{12.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
Component & Correspondence\\
\midrule
Signed pairs & the sign is exactly $\pm1$; the interval is the ordered pair $\min/\max$ of $qm$ and $qm+\sigma$; both denominators are positive (\texttt{signedPair}, \texttt{wSigned}).\\
Multiplier range & $R(q)\le m\le8q^\varepsilon$ with $V$, $E$, $R$ as in \eqref{eq:envelope} (\texttt{Venv}, \texttt{Eenv}, \texttt{Rq}); the envelope thresholds are eventual statements (\texttt{venv\_le}, \texttt{Eenv\_le\_eventually}).\\

Position orientation & the bit chooses left or right relative to the multiple of four (\texttt{slot}), not a uniform sign.\\

The paper and the formalization prove the same covering lemma with different intermediate estimates. The formalization uses an explicit fixed power loss in the divisor-count step: the lemma \texttt{paper\_steps\_4\_5} (file \texttt{Erdos289/Lemma3Steps45.lean}) proves, from the retained-set estimate $|J|\ge q^{7\varepsilon/8}/2$ and a divisor majorant of the form $\max\{n_0,x^{\varepsilon/4000}\}$,
\[
\Vgap\ge\frac{q^{1-d\varepsilon/8-d\varepsilon/500}}{(16Cd)^d}.
\]
This differs from the divisor-envelope bound \eqref{eq:G4}, which uses $|J|\ge3m/4$ and $\Delta_C(q)$, and neither bound is claimed to be the other; but it is sufficient for the same rank conclusion. For $d\ge2$ its exponent exceeds the upper-bound exponent of \eqref{eq:G2} by
\[
\varepsilon\Bigl(\frac{3d-4}8-\frac d{500}\Bigr)>0,
\]
which at $d=2$ is $\varepsilon(1/4-1/250)$ and increases with $d$; for $d=1$ the progression-length exponent is $1+(3/8-1/500)\varepsilon>1$. Both positivity claims are uniform in the bounded rank $d\le d_0$. The signed descent uses the common mass bound \eqref{eq:D6} summed as in \eqref{eq:D7}, giving $120L^{-1/40}$ in both presentations. Other harmless differences: the formal odd-prime construction inverts $4t$ rather than solving $4rt\equiv-1$, which exchanges the two candidates; the formal first sieve uses $\eta=\varepsilon/20000$ with allowance $M_q/200$; the orientation count averages $(81/256)^{B_q}$ rather than $2^{-B_q}$; the density proof splits the labels at a smaller exponent; the cancellation uses a product denominator rather than a least common multiple; and the auxiliary avoidance condition is stronger than the displayed distance two. None of these changes the terminal target or adds an axiom, and the proof scripts are not a line-for-line translation of every displayed estimate.
